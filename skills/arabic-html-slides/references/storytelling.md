## The governing message

Write one sentence that the audience should be able to repeat tomorrow. It names a conclusion and, where relevant, the action.

| Weak (a topic) | Strong (a message) |
|---|---|
| نتائج الربع الثالث | بلغنا مستهدف العام قبل موعده، والتحدّي الآن الجودة لا العدد |
| خطة التحول الرقمي | نقترح البدء بثلاث خدمات تمثّل 70% من المعاملات |
| Market overview | The market doubled in two years and nobody owns the mid-tier yet |

If you cannot write it, the deck is not ready to be designed. Ask the user what they want the audience to decide or believe.

## Three structures that cover most decks

**Answer first** (decisions, steering committees, senior audiences)

1. The answer or recommendation
2. Three or so supporting reasons, one section each, each with evidence
3. What it costs and what could go wrong
4. The decision requested

**Situation, complication, resolution** (proposals, change, strategy)

1. Where we are: a situation the audience recognises
2. What changed or what is wrong: the complication
3. The question that raises
4. The answer and how it works
5. Next steps

**Journey** (keynotes, launches, teaching)

1. A hook: a number, a question, a scene
2. The problem as people feel it
3. The idea
4. Proof: demo, case, data
5. What it means for this audience
6. A close that echoes the hook

Senior audiences in government and large organisations expect the answer early. Do not hold the conclusion for a reveal on slide 20.

## Titles that carry the argument

Every content slide has a title that is a full sentence stating that slide's point. Listed alone, the titles should read as a summary of the talk.

| Label title | Action title |
|---|---|
| نسبة الإتمام حسب المسار | مسار الأمن السيبراني وحده دون مستهدف الإتمام |
| أسباب الانسحاب | السبب في التصميم لا في المتدرّبين |
| الجدول الزمني | المسار الجديد جاهز مع دفعة يناير |
| Budget | The redesign needs no additional budget this year |

Arabic titles:

- Prefer a verbal sentence that leads with the finding: «ارتفع…»، «يتركّز…»، «نقترح…». A nominal sentence is fine when it is sharper: «السبب في التصميم».
- Twelve words at most. Two lines at most at display size.
- No colon-and-list titles, no trailing full stop.
- Section kickers above the title can be labels: «الأداء»، «التشخيص»، «المقترح».

## Density

- One idea per slide. A second idea is a second slide.
- A presented slide: about 30 words beyond the title. A read-alone slide: up to about 70. The linter flags slides above 75.
- Three to five items in any list. Seven is a document, not a slide.
- One chart per slide, with the takeaway in the title and the key series highlighted.
- Move detail, methodology and backup tables to an appendix after the closing slide.

Rough pacing: two minutes per content slide for a briefing, one minute for a keynote. A 15-minute slot is 7 to 9 slides, not 20.

## Executive conventions in Arabic

- Open with a title slide carrying the subject, the audience or occasion, and the date. Institutional decks often show the organisation's identity here; follow the user's brand rules and do not invent logos.
- Honorifics and formal address belong in the spoken delivery and in the notes, not as slide text, unless the user asks for them.
- Tone is direct and measured. Avoid marketing superlatives («الأفضل»، «غير مسبوق») unless backed by a number on the same slide.
- State sources for figures in a small line at the bottom: «المصدر: …، حتى 30 سبتمبر 2026».
- End with what is requested: «المطلوب»، «القرارات المطلوبة»، «الخطوات التالية». A slide that only says «شكرًا» wastes the moment when attention is highest; put the request there and thank the audience aloud.
- Dates: write the month name. Where the audience expects it, show Hijri alongside Gregorian.

## Numbers on slides

- Round to what the audience needs: «12,400» or «أكثر من 12 ألفًا», not «12,397».
- Put the comparison next to the number: against target, against last period, against a peer.
- A number alone on a slide needs a noun and a verdict: «87% نسبة الإتمام، أعلى بـ 9 نقاط من الربع الثاني».
- Keep units consistent across slides and languages. If Arabic says «مليون ريال», English says "SAR million", with the same figure.
- Use one digit system throughout.

## Bilingual decks

- Decide the mode: one deck that toggles (the starter does this), or both languages visible on each slide. Toggle is better for presenting. Both-visible suits printed handouts and mixed audiences, but halves the space: cut the text to half.
- Write each language natively from the same storyline. Translate the meaning of the title, not its word order.
- English titles are usually longer than Arabic ones in characters and shorter in line count; check both for wrapping.
- Keep proper nouns, product names and figures identical in both.

## Speaker notes
